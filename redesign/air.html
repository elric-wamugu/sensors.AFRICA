Why air, first

The quiet emergency in every breath

Air pollution is linked to roughly one in nine deaths worldwide — about seven million premature deaths every year. The particles that do the damage are the ones you cannot see: PM2.5 slips past the body's defences into the bloodstream. Across much of Africa, the street-level data needed to fight it barely exists. That is the gap this network was built to close.

36% of lung-cancer deaths are linked to air pollution
35% of deaths from COPD are linked to air pollution
34% of stroke deaths are linked to air pollution
27% of heart-disease deaths are linked to air pollution

Under the hood

A laser, a fan, and a Wi-Fi chip

Every air reading on this page comes from an SDS011 particulate sensor — the workhorse of the open-source Luftdaten citizen-sensing movement. A small fan draws air through a chamber where a laser beam scatters off airborne particles; from that scatter the sensor counts what's floating in the air and reports PM2.5 and PM10 concentrations in µg/m³ every few minutes.

The sensor rides on a low-cost microcontroller that pushes each reading over Wi-Fi — or GSM where connectivity is thin — to the open sensors.AFRICA platform, where it is mapped, archived and published. The whole station costs about as much as a pair of shoes, mounts on a window ledge, and is assembled by the citizens who host it. That is the point: cheap enough for a household, honest enough for a newsroom.
